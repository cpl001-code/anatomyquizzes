Study notes

Lateral pterygoid muscle

Origin
Upper head: infratemporal surface of the greater wing of the sphenoid bone. Lower head: lateral surface of the lateral pterygoid plate.
Insertion
Pterygoid fovea on the neck of the mandible, and the capsule and articular disc of the temporomandibular joint.
Innervation
Nerve to lateral pterygoid, a branch of the mandibular nerve (V3).
Action
Pulls the head of the mandible and the articular disc forward, which pushes the jaw forward and helps open the mouth. One side alone swings the chin to the opposite side, as in grinding.

High yield

  • With a lesion of the mandibular nerve (V3) on one side, the jaw deviates toward the weak side when the mouth is opened, pushed across by the healthy lateral pterygoid of the other side.

Medial pterygoid muscle

Origin
Deep head: medial surface of the lateral pterygoid plate and the pyramidal process of the palatine bone. Superficial head: tuberosity of the maxilla.
Insertion
Medial surface of the ramus and angle of the mandible, below the mandibular foramen.
Innervation
Nerve to medial pterygoid, a branch of the mandibular nerve (V3).
Action
Raises the mandible to close the jaw and helps push it forward. One side alone, working with the lateral pterygoid of the same side, swings the chin to the opposite side, as in grinding.

Masseter muscle

Origin
Lower border and inner surface of the zygomatic arch.
Insertion
Lateral surface of the ramus and angle of the mandible.
Innervation
Masseteric nerve, a branch of the mandibular nerve (V3).
Action
Raises the mandible to close the jaw. Its superficial fibers also help push the jaw forward.

High yield

  • One of the four muscles of mastication, all from the 1st pharyngeal arch and all supplied by V3. Three close the jaw (Masseter, teMporalis, Medial pterygoid: the M's munch) and the lateral pterygoid lowers it.

Mandible

Location
Lower jaw. It is the only freely movable bone of the skull.
Articulates with
Temporal bones, at the two temporomandibular joints.
Features
Body, angle, and ramus. Coronoid and condylar processes. The mandibular foramen, where the inferior alveolar nerve enters, and the mental foramen.

Sphenoid bone

Location
Center of the skull base, behind the orbits. It is shaped like a butterfly.
Articulates with
Frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, and ethmoid bones, and the zygomatic and palatine bones and the vomer.
Features
Body, greater and lesser wings, and pterygoid processes. The body has the sphenoidal sinuses and the sella turcica, which holds the pituitary gland.

High yield

  • Its openings and what passes through: optic canal (CN II, ophthalmic artery), superior orbital fissure (CN III, IV, V1, VI), foramen rotundum (V2), foramen ovale (V3), foramen spinosum (middle meningeal artery).

Temporal bone

Location
Side and base of the skull, around the ear.
Articulates with
Parietal, occipital, sphenoid, and zygomatic bones, and the mandible at the temporomandibular joint.
Features
Squamous, petrous, mastoid, and tympanic parts and the styloid process. The petrous part houses the middle and inner ear.

High yield

  • At the pterion the frontal, parietal, temporal, and sphenoid bones meet and the skull is thin. A fracture here can tear the middle meningeal artery and cause an epidural hematoma.
  • Its openings and what passes through: internal acoustic meatus (CN VII and VIII), stylomastoid foramen (CN VII leaves the skull), and, between it and the occipital bone, the jugular foramen (CN IX, X, XI, and the internal jugular vein).

Maxilla

Location
Upper jaw. It forms most of the hard palate, the floor of the orbit, and the side wall of the nasal cavity.
Articulates with
Frontal, nasal, lacrimal, ethmoid, zygomatic, and palatine bones, the vomer, the inferior nasal concha, and the opposite maxilla.
Features
Maxillary sinus, infraorbital foramen, and the alveolar process that carries the upper teeth.

Palatine bone

Location
L-shaped bone at the back of the nasal cavity, between the maxilla and the sphenoid. Its horizontal plate forms the back of the hard palate and its perpendicular plate part of the lateral wall of the nasal cavity.
Articulates with
Maxilla, sphenoid, ethmoid, inferior nasal concha, vomer, and the palatine bone of the other side.
Features
Greater and lesser palatine foramina, for the nerves and vessels of the same names. The sphenopalatine notch at the top of the perpendicular plate becomes the sphenopalatine foramen, which leads from the pterygopalatine fossa into the nasal cavity.

Vomer

Location
Thin plate of bone in the midline that forms the lower, back part of the nasal septum.
Articulates with
Sphenoid, the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid, both maxillae, and both palatine bones.
