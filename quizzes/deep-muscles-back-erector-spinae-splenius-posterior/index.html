Study notes

Iliocostalis lumborum muscle

Origin
Common tendon of the erector spinae: iliac crest, posterior surface of the sacrum, and the sacral and lower lumbar spinous processes.
Insertion
Angles of the lower six or seven ribs.
Innervation
Posterior rami of spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the vertebral column. One side alone bends the trunk to that side.

High yield

  • Erector spinae columns from lateral to medial: Iliocostalis, Longissimus, Spinalis ("I Love Spine").

Iliocostalis thoracis muscle

Origin
Angles of the lower six ribs, medial to the insertion of iliocostalis lumborum.
Insertion
Angles of the upper six ribs and the transverse process of C7.
Innervation
Posterior rami of spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the thoracic spine. One side alone bends the trunk to that side.

Longissimus mm.

Origin
Thoracis: common tendon of the erector spinae and lumbar transverse processes. Cervicis: transverse processes of T1 to T5. Capitis: transverse processes of T1 to T5 and articular processes of C4 to C7.
Insertion
Thoracis: transverse processes of the thoracic vertebrae and the lower ribs between their tubercles and angles. Cervicis: transverse processes of C2 to C6. Capitis: mastoid process of the temporal bone.
Innervation
Posterior rami of spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the vertebral column and the head. One side alone bends the trunk and neck to that side; longissimus capitis turns the face to the same side.

Spinalis mm.

Origin
Thoracis: spinous processes of T11 to L2. Cervicis: lower part of the ligamentum nuchae and the spinous process of C7.
Insertion
Thoracis: spinous processes of the upper thoracic vertebrae. Cervicis: spinous process of the axis (C2).
Innervation
Posterior rami of spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the vertebral column. One side alone helps bend it to that side.

Semispinalis capitis muscle

Origin
Transverse processes of C7 to T6 and articular processes of C4 to C6.
Insertion
Occipital bone, between the superior and inferior nuchal lines.
Innervation
Posterior rami of cervical spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the head and neck. One side alone helps bend the head to that side.

High yield

  • The greater occipital nerve (posterior ramus of C2) pierces semispinalis capitis on its way to the scalp; entrapment here is one cause of occipital neuralgia.
  • Forms the roof of the suboccipital triangle.

Splenius capitis muscle

Origin
Lower half of the ligamentum nuchae and spinous processes of C7 to T3.
Insertion
Mastoid process of the temporal bone and the lateral third of the superior nuchal line of the occipital bone.
Innervation
Posterior rami of cervical spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the head and neck. One side alone bends the head to that side and turns the face to the same side.

Splenius cervicis muscle

Origin
Spinous processes of T3 to T6.
Insertion
Transverse processes of C1 to C3.
Innervation
Posterior rami of cervical spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the neck. One side alone bends the neck to that side and turns it to the same side.

Serratus posterior superior muscle

Origin
Lower part of the ligamentum nuchae and spinous processes of C7 to T3.
Insertion
Upper borders of ribs 2 to 5, lateral to their angles.
Innervation
Anterior rami: intercostal nerves 2 to 5.
Action
Raises ribs 2 to 5. Traditionally called a muscle of inspiration; its real role may be mostly proprioceptive.

Serratus posterior inferior muscle

Origin
Spinous processes of T11 to L2, by way of the thoracolumbar fascia.
Insertion
Lower borders of ribs 9 to 12, lateral to their angles.
Innervation
Anterior rami of T9 to T12 (lower intercostal nerves and the subcostal nerve).
Action
Pulls ribs 9 to 12 down and back. Traditionally called a muscle of forced expiration; its real role may be mostly proprioceptive.

Semispinalis mm.

Origin
Thoracis: transverse processes of T6 to T10. Cervicis: transverse processes of T1 to T6.
Insertion
Thoracis: spinous processes of C6 to T4. Cervicis: spinous processes of C2 to C5.
Innervation
Posterior rami of spinal nerves.
Action
Both sides together extend the thoracic and cervical spine. One side alone turns the trunk and neck toward the opposite side.
