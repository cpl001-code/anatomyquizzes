Study notes

Stomach

Location
Upper left part of the abdomen, between the esophagus and the duodenum.
Function
Stores and churns food. Its parietal cells secrete acid and intrinsic factor and its chief cells secrete pepsinogen.
Blood supply
All from the celiac trunk: left and right gastric arteries along the lesser curvature, left and right gastro-omental arteries along the greater curvature, and short gastric arteries to the fundus.

High yield

  • Without intrinsic factor (pernicious anemia, or after removal of the stomach) the normal absorption of vitamin B12 in the terminal ileum fails and deficiency follows. Only about 1% of an oral dose is still absorbed, passively.

Duodenum

Location
C-shaped first part of the small intestine, curving around the head of the pancreas from the pylorus to the jejunum. Most of it is fixed to the back wall of the abdomen.
Function
Receives food from the stomach, and bile and pancreatic juice at the major duodenal papilla in its second part.
Blood supply
Branches of the celiac trunk above the major papilla and of the superior mesenteric artery below it.

High yield

  • An ulcer in the posterior wall of its first part can erode the gastroduodenal artery and bleed; an ulcer in the anterior wall can perforate into the abdominal cavity.
  • Duodenal atresia in a newborn shows a double bubble on X-ray and is associated with Down syndrome.

Pancreas

Location
Lies across the back wall of the abdomen behind the stomach. Its head sits in the curve of the duodenum and its tail reaches the spleen.
Function
Exocrine part: digestive enzymes and bicarbonate, released into the duodenum. Endocrine part (islets): insulin, glucagon, and somatostatin.

High yield

  • A cancer of the head of the pancreas can block the bile duct and cause painless jaundice, sometimes with a painless gallbladder that can be felt (Courvoisier sign).

Spleen

Location
Upper left part of the abdomen, against the diaphragm and under ribs 9 to 11.
Function
Filters the blood, removes old red cells and bacteria, and takes part in immune responses.
Blood supply
Splenic artery, a branch of the celiac trunk. The splenic vein drains into the hepatic portal vein.

High yield

  • After removal of the spleen there is a lasting risk of severe infection with encapsulated bacteria (Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Neisseria meningitidis).
  • It is often injured in blunt trauma to the left lower ribs, and bleeding from it can cause pain in the left shoulder (Kehr sign).

Gallbladder

Location
In a fossa on the lower surface of the liver. Its fundus reaches the edge of the liver at the tip of the right 9th costal cartilage.
Function
Stores and concentrates bile and releases it into the duodenum when cholecystokinin makes it contract.
Blood supply
Cystic artery, usually a branch of the right hepatic artery.

High yield

  • In acute cholecystitis, pressing under the right ribs stops the patient from breathing in (Murphy sign), and the pain may be felt in the right shoulder.

Esophagus

Location
Runs from the pharynx at C6 down through the chest, behind the trachea and the left atrium, and through the diaphragm at T10 to the stomach.
Function
Carries food to the stomach by waves of contraction. Its upper third is striated muscle and its lower third smooth muscle.

High yield

  • Its lower end is a portosystemic anastomosis, where esophageal varices form in portal hypertension.
  • Long-standing acid reflux changes the lining of its lower end to intestinal-type epithelium (Barrett esophagus), which can turn into adenocarcinoma.

Bile duct

Location
Formed by the cystic duct and the common hepatic duct. It runs down in the hepatoduodenal ligament, behind the first part of the duodenum, and through the head of the pancreas.
Function
Carries bile to the duodenum. It joins the main pancreatic duct and opens at the major duodenal papilla.

High yield

  • A gallstone or a tumor of the head of the pancreas that blocks it causes jaundice with pale stools and dark urine. A stone lodged at its lower end can also cause pancreatitis.

Abdominal aorta

Arises from
Continues the thoracic aorta as it passes through the diaphragm at T12.
Course
Descends in front of the lumbar vertebrae, on the left of the inferior vena cava, and divides at L4 into the two common iliac arteries.
Supplies
Three unpaired branches to the gut (celiac trunk, superior mesenteric, and inferior mesenteric arteries) and paired branches to the suprarenal glands, kidneys, gonads, and body wall.

High yield

  • Abdominal aortic aneurysms usually lie below the renal arteries. The main risk factors are smoking, age over 65, male sex, and hypertension.

Inferior vena cava

Drains
Lower limbs, pelvis, abdominal wall, kidneys, suprarenal glands, gonads, and liver. It is formed at L5 by the two common iliac veins.
Course
Ascends on the right of the aorta and behind the liver and passes through the diaphragm at T8.
Empties into
Right atrium.

High yield

  • Levels of the openings in the diaphragm: inferior vena cava at T8, esophagus at T10, aorta at T12 (I ate ten eggs at twelve).

Hepatic portal vein

Drains
Stomach, intestines, pancreas, spleen, and gallbladder. It is formed behind the neck of the pancreas by the superior mesenteric and splenic veins.
Course
Ascends in the hepatoduodenal ligament, behind the bile duct and the proper hepatic artery.
Empties into
The sinusoids of the liver.

High yield

  • In portal hypertension blood bypasses the liver through portosystemic anastomoses: esophageal varices (left gastric vein to esophageal veins), caput medusae (paraumbilical veins to epigastric veins), and anorectal varices (superior rectal vein to middle and inferior rectal veins).

Celiac trunk

Arises from
Front of the aorta at T12, just below the diaphragm.
Supplies
The foregut, from the lower esophagus to the middle of the duodenum, with the liver, gallbladder, and pancreas, and the spleen. Its three branches are the left gastric, splenic, and common hepatic arteries.

Common hepatic artery

Arises from
Celiac trunk.
Course
Runs to the right along the upper border of the pancreas, gives off the gastroduodenal artery, and continues as the proper hepatic artery.
Supplies
Liver and gallbladder and, through the gastroduodenal artery and the right gastric artery (a branch of the proper hepatic artery), the stomach, duodenum, and pancreas.

High yield

  • The gastroduodenal artery runs behind the first part of the duodenum, so an ulcer of its posterior wall can erode the artery and bleed heavily.

Superior mesenteric artery

Arises from
Front of the aorta at L1, behind the neck of the pancreas.
Course
Passes in front of the left renal vein and the third part of the duodenum and enters the mesentery.
Supplies
The midgut, from the lower duodenum to the first two thirds of the transverse colon.

High yield

  • Superior mesenteric artery syndrome: after marked weight loss the third part of the duodenum is squeezed between this artery and the aorta.

Kidney

Location
On the back wall of the abdomen behind the peritoneum, from about T12 to L3. The right kidney lies a little lower than the left.
Function
Filters the blood and makes urine; controls water, salts, acid-base balance, and blood pressure; makes erythropoietin and activates vitamin D.
Blood supply
Renal artery from the aorta. The renal vein drains into the inferior vena cava.

High yield

  • The left kidney is preferred for transplantation from a living donor because its renal vein is longer.
  • A horseshoe kidney is held low in the abdomen because it is caught under the inferior mesenteric artery as it ascends.

Right suprarenal gland

Location
On the upper pole of each kidney.
Function
Cortex, from outside in: aldosterone (zona glomerulosa), cortisol (zona fasciculata), androgens (zona reticularis). Medulla: epinephrine and norepinephrine.
Blood supply
Superior, middle, and inferior suprarenal arteries. The right suprarenal vein drains into the inferior vena cava and the left into the left renal vein.

High yield

  • The layers of the cortex and their products, from outside in: salt, sugar, sex.
  • The medulla develops from neural crest cells; a pheochromocytoma is a tumor of its chromaffin cells.
