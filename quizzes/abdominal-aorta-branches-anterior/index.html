Study notes

Abdominal aorta

Arises from
Continues the thoracic aorta as it passes through the diaphragm at T12.
Course
Descends in front of the lumbar vertebrae, on the left of the inferior vena cava, and divides at L4 into the two common iliac arteries.
Supplies
Three unpaired branches to the gut (celiac trunk, superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric arteries) and paired branches to the suprarenal glands, kidneys, gonads and body wall.

High yield

  • Abdominal aortic aneurysms usually lie below the renal arteries. The main risk factors are smoking, age over 65, male sex and hypertension.

Celiac trunk

Arises from
Front of the aorta at T12, just below the diaphragm.
Supplies
The foregut, from the lower esophagus to the middle of the duodenum, with the liver, gallbladder and pancreas, and the spleen. Its three branches are the left gastric, splenic and common hepatic arteries.

Superior mesenteric artery

Arises from
Front of the aorta at L1, behind the neck of the pancreas.
Course
Passes in front of the left renal vein and the third part of the duodenum and enters the mesentery.
Supplies
The midgut, from the lower duodenum to the first two thirds of the transverse colon.

High yield

  • Superior mesenteric artery syndrome: after marked weight loss the third part of the duodenum is squeezed between this artery and the aorta.

Inferior mesenteric artery

Arises from
Front of the aorta at L3.
Supplies
The hindgut, from the last third of the transverse colon to the rectum, as far down as the pectinate line of the anal canal, through the left colic, sigmoid and superior rectal arteries.

High yield

  • The splenic flexure lies at the border between the superior and inferior mesenteric territories (a watershed area), so it is vulnerable to ischemia when blood pressure falls.

Renal artery

Arises from
Side of the aorta at about L1 to L2, just below the superior mesenteric artery.
Course
The right artery is longer and passes behind the inferior vena cava. At the hilum each artery lies behind its renal vein.
Supplies
Kidney, with small branches to the suprarenal gland and the ureter.

High yield

  • Renal artery stenosis causes hypertension through renin release. It is due to atherosclerosis in older men and to fibromuscular dysplasia in young women.

Testicular artery

Arises from
Front of the aorta at about L2, below the renal arteries.
Course
Descends on psoas major, crosses in front of the ureter and runs through the inguinal canal in the spermatic cord.
Supplies
Testis and epididymis.

Common iliac artery

Arises from
The division of the abdominal aorta at L4.
Course
Runs down and laterally and divides in front of the sacroiliac joint into the external and internal iliac arteries.
Supplies
Pelvis and lower limb, through its two branches.

High yield

  • The right common iliac artery can compress the left common iliac vein against the spine, which predisposes to deep vein thrombosis of the left leg (May-Thurner syndrome).

External iliac artery

Arises from
Common iliac artery, in front of the sacroiliac joint.
Course
Runs along the medial border of psoas major and passes under the inguinal ligament, where it becomes the femoral artery.
Supplies
Lower limb. Its inferior epigastric and deep circumflex iliac branches supply the lower anterior abdominal wall.

High yield

  • An indirect inguinal hernia leaves the abdomen lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels; a direct hernia bulges medial to them.

Internal iliac artery

Arises from
Common iliac artery, in front of the sacroiliac joint.
Course
Descends into the pelvis and divides into an anterior and a posterior division.
Supplies
Pelvic organs, the perineum, the gluteal region and the medial side of the thigh.

Inferior vena cava

Drains
Lower limbs, pelvis, abdominal wall, kidneys, suprarenal glands, gonads and liver. It is formed at L5 by the two common iliac veins.
Course
Ascends on the right of the aorta and behind the liver and passes through the diaphragm at T8.
Empties into
Right atrium.

High yield

  • Levels of the openings in the diaphragm: inferior vena cava at T8, esophagus at T10, aorta at T12 (I ate ten eggs at twelve).

Common hepatic artery

Arises from
Celiac trunk.
Course
Runs to the right along the upper border of the pancreas, gives off the gastroduodenal artery and continues as the proper hepatic artery.
Supplies
Liver and gallbladder and, through the gastroduodenal artery and the right gastric artery (a branch of the proper hepatic artery), the stomach, duodenum and pancreas.

High yield

  • The gastroduodenal artery runs behind the first part of the duodenum, so an ulcer of its posterior wall can erode the artery and bleed heavily.

Left gastric artery

Arises from
Celiac trunk, as its smallest branch.
Course
Ascends to the upper end of the stomach and then runs along the lesser curvature to meet the right gastric artery.
Supplies
Lesser curvature of the stomach and the lower esophagus.

High yield

  • A gastric ulcer on the lesser curvature can erode this artery and bleed.

Splenic artery

Arises from
Celiac trunk, as its largest branch.
Course
Follows a winding course to the left along the upper border of the pancreas, behind the stomach, to the hilum of the spleen.
Supplies
Spleen, the body and tail of the pancreas and, through the short gastric and left gastro-omental arteries, the fundus and greater curvature of the stomach.

Median sacral artery

Arises from
Back of the aorta, just above its division.
Course
Descends in the midline in front of L4, L5, the sacrum and the coccyx.
Supplies
Lower lumbar vertebrae, sacrum and coccyx.

Renal vein

Drains
Kidney. The left vein also receives the left suprarenal and left gonadal veins.
Course
Lies in front of the renal artery. The left vein is longer and crosses in front of the aorta just below the superior mesenteric artery.
Empties into
Inferior vena cava.

High yield

  • The left renal vein can be squeezed between the superior mesenteric artery and the aorta (nutcracker syndrome), causing flank pain, blood in the urine and a left varicocele.
  • The left gonadal vein drains into the left renal vein and the right one directly into the inferior vena cava, which is why a varicocele is usually on the left.

Kidney

Location
On the back wall of the abdomen behind the peritoneum, from about T12 to L3. The right kidney lies a little lower than the left.
Function
Filters the blood and makes urine; controls water, salts, acid-base balance and blood pressure; makes erythropoietin and activates vitamin D.
Blood supply
Renal artery from the aorta. The renal vein drains into the inferior vena cava.

High yield

  • The left kidney is preferred for transplantation from a living donor because its renal vein is longer.
  • A horseshoe kidney is held low in the abdomen because it is caught under the inferior mesenteric artery as it ascends.

Suprarenal gland

Location
On the upper pole of each kidney.
Function
Cortex, from outside in: aldosterone (zona glomerulosa), cortisol (zona fasciculata), androgens (zona reticularis). Medulla: epinephrine and norepinephrine.
Blood supply
Superior, middle and inferior suprarenal arteries. The right suprarenal vein drains into the inferior vena cava and the left into the left renal vein.

High yield

  • The layers of the cortex and their products, from outside in: salt, sugar, sex.
  • The medulla develops from neural crest cells; a pheochromocytoma is a tumor of its chromaffin cells.
